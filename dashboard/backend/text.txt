High benchmark scores do not establish that a deepfake detector will generalise or that an added frequency pathway caused an improvement. This study evaluates a video-level detector that combines frozen EfficientNet-B4 spatial features with a lightweight two-dimensional Fourier branch through a learned additive gate. FaceForensics++ was split by connected source-pair components, preventing related source material from crossing training, validation and test partitions. On 738 test videos, spatial and dual models achieved AUCs of 0.9884 and 0.9901; a paired cluster bootstrap placed the improvement at 0.0005-0.0031. Disabling only the trained frequency residual reduced AUC to 0.9663, whereas a separately trained frequency-only baseline achieved 0.6439. Zero-shot dual-model AUC was 0.8786 on the evaluable Celeb-DF collection and 0.8362 on its official test list. Calibration, robustness, sampling, latency and selective-referral experiments expose important limitations under shift. A Flask-React interface presents these outputs as auditable screening evidence rather than calibrated probability or forensic proof.
